Introduction

Why this project exists

Next-generation sequencing (NGS) produces huge genomic datasets. Comparative genomics asks how genomes resemble and differ across species.

Two complementary indices from the brief:

IndexWhat it tells usUses
GC ContentNucleotide composition; linked to DNA melting, codon preference, mutation context, evolutionary adaptationCompositional comparison
K-mer analysisShort DNA-word frequencies without alignmentPhylogenomics (exploratory), classification, assembly, metagenomics, species ID
Goal: compare similarities and differences among five organisms using GC, k-mers, statistics, clustering, and motif scanning — with honest limits when data cannot answer a question.

Objectives

Project objectives (from the brief)

  • Download / ingest genomes from trusted databases
  • Calculate and compare GC Content
  • Extract k-mer frequencies
  • Study relationships with statistics & machine learning (PCA, clustering)
  • Visualize with scientific plots
  • Interpret biological meaning

Languages allowed: Python (our choice), R, or others. AI-assisted coding was permitted; interpretation is ours.

§2 GC — Concept

Section 2 — Nucleotide composition (GC Content)

GC% = ((G + C) / (A + T + G + C)) × 100

Ambiguous bases (N, …) are excluded from the denominator.

Biological importance (brief): DNA stability, melting temperature, gene expression context, chromatin, codon preference, mutation rate. G–C has three H-bonds vs two for A–T — often discussed as contributing to local duplex stability, but whole-genome “stability” is multifactorial.

§2 Q4 Size vs GC

§2 Question 4 — Genome size vs GC?

Answer: In this sample of five genomes, the relationship is weak and not statistically significant.

  • Pearson r ≈ −0.25, p ≈ 0.69
  • Spearman ρ ≈ −0.30, p ≈ 0.62
With n = 5 we conclude “insufficient evidence for a linear/monotonic link in this set,” not “no relationship exists in nature.”

§2 Q5 Chromosome GC

§2 Question 5 — Do chromosomes within a species share GC?

Answer: On main chromosomes, GC is relatively similar within a species (low SD). Including unplaced/organellar records increases heterogeneity (especially N. crassa).

§3 K-mer — Concept

Section 3 — K-mer analysis

A k-mer is every overlapping DNA substring of length k.

Example: ATGCGA, k=3 → ATG, TGC, GCG, CGA.

Uses (brief): genome classification, species ID, assembly, metagenomics, evolutionary studies, ML features.

  • K-mer diversity: unique/total — higher can indicate more complexity (context-dependent)
  • Shannon entropy: evenness of the frequency distribution; uniform usage → higher entropy

We counted overlapping k-mers for k = 3, 4, 5; multivariate comparison uses normalized k = 4 vectors (256 dimensions).

§3 Q7 Evolutionary

§3 Question 7 — Do similar k-mers imply evolutionary closeness?

Answer: Partially / descriptively yes — with caveats.

  • N. crassa and Y. lipolytica have highly similar 4-mer profiles, cluster early (Ward), and sit close in PCA.
  • That is consistent with fungal relatedness.
  • A compositional dendrogram is not a formal phylogenetic tree.

§4 Similarity — Methods

Section 4 — Genome similarity from k-mers

Each genome → normalized 4-mer frequency vector → compare:

  • Euclidean distance: smaller ⇒ more similar
  • Cosine similarity: ~1 similar direction; ~0 dissimilar

Then: heatmaps · PCA · hierarchical clustering (Ward) · dendrogram.

§6 TATA — Concept & Method

Section 6 — TATA Box

A TATA box is a regulatory motif in gene promoters, typically ~25–35 bp upstream of the transcription start site (TSS), often near TATAAA.

Method we used: scan canonical TATAAA in the first 500 bp of each pooled genomic FASTA.
Not annotated gene promoters (no GFF/GTF gene set).

Discussion

Discussion — unifying story

  • Composition: Yarrowia GC-high; Dictyostelium AT-rich/low GC.
  • K-mers: fungi closest; Dictyostelium farthest from yeast-like profiles.
  • Stats: entropy (and GC) differ among species; size–diversity weak; GC–entropy strong in n=5.
  • Limits: brief mismatch · no plant · one animal · short-k saturation · nested tests · TATA window ≠ genes.

Conclusion

Conclusion

  1. We built a reproducible comparative genomics study on five real assemblies.
  2. GC captures nucleotide composition; k-mers capture short-sequence usage and enable alignment-free clustering.
  3. Together they give a coherent compositional picture — with transparent limits.
  4. Foundation for deeper work: annotated promoters, expression, longer k, formal phylogenomics.
